This reference defines a compact structure for durable research synthesis. Use it flexibly, omit empty sections, and keep entries linked to authoritative evidence.

# Research ledger schema

## Objective and evaluation criteria

State the research question, relevant platforms, and what evidence would answer it.

## Baseline knowledge and expectations

List ideas that were already expected before the current investigation. This prevents later validation from being presented as a new conceptual discovery.

## Current claims

Give each durable claim a stable ID. Include:

- **Status:** expected, confirmed, new, tentative, contradicted, or superseded
- **Scope:** simulator model, FPGA configuration, Linux workload, or other precise boundary
- **Claim:** one falsifiable statement
- **Evidence:** exact artifact, commit, trace, or accepted measurement
- **Meaning:** why the evidence changes a decision or understanding
- **Limits:** what the evidence does not establish

## Superseded or rejected findings

Retain the original claim or number, its source, why it was rejected or narrowed, and the accepted replacement.

## Open questions

For each question, record why it matters, what is already known, and the cheapest decisive experiment. Distinguish unavailable external evidence from work that can proceed locally.

## Next experiment

Name the single highest-value next experiment, the claim it can change, required recovery or setup, controls, and acceptance criteria.

## Delta log

Record only synthesis changes: claim added, strengthened, narrowed, contradicted, or superseded. Do not duplicate the chronological engineering work log.
