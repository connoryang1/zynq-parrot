Correct address mapping
Linux is loaded physically at 0x80200000 and linked virtually at
0xffffffff80000000. For a kernel-table physical address P, resolve virtual
address 0xffffffff80000000 + (P - 0x80200000). The trace itself validates this
offset: the scheduler trigger at virtual 0xffffffff8081babe maps to physical
address 0x80a1babe (cache line 0x80a1ba80), adjacent to the recorded
post-trigger scheduler-path line 0x80a1bac0.

Exact replay-disabled trace
The accepted one-leg FPGA capture used the same persistent-futex capture ELF
as the earlier instruction experiment, but the exact joint kernel. It records
222 I-side and 15 D-side accepted misses over 23,386 physical cycles, with no
overflow, TRACE_PASS, runner exit zero, CORE PASS, and BSG PASS. None of the
16 old-table physical lines appears in the 222-entry I-side history. The old
experiment observed 11 of its 16 configured lines in a complete disabled
fast-path sample, so the zero overlap is a direct exact-binary coverage loss.

Correction to discarded first pass
An initial local audit incorrectly treated physical kernel addresses as if
their low bits were linked virtual addresses, omitting the 2 MiB load offset.
That produced false filesystem symbol names. The trace's known scheduler CSR
location exposed the mistake before commit; those mappings and conclusions
are rejected and replaced by this file.

Conclusion
The old-16 table is not useful for the exact joint-kernel capture path: zero of
its entries are demanded. RL-068 therefore does not test a trained instruction
best case, even though its data controls and timing remain valid. Train a new
table from an exact-kernel replay-disabled trace and resolve every candidate
with the corrected load offset before FPGA use.
